Every feature, step by step

One account opening, seen from every seat — the customer on their phone, the relationship manager at the branch, the maker and verifier on a bulk batch, the operations analyst clearing exceptions, management and the presenter. Pick a persona to get their flows as numbered steps, each with a screenshot of the real screen and what you should see.

How to use this guide

  • Start the app with npm run dev (port 5180) and open the launcher. Every “Open it” button in this guide opens the right surface; none of the apps can open a single case from a URL, so the first step of each flow says how to reach the case.
  • Use the sample cases below: each sits at a different stage of the Maestro case, so every flow has something to try it on. The store reseeds itself every 12 hours, and “Reset demo data” on the launcher reseeds it now.
  • What is live: the app talks to the real UiPath staging tenant. Submitting an application, uploading a form, “Run in Maestro”, “Approve … & advance”, “Confirm and continue” and Batches’ “Process N applications” start or move real Maestro cases. The steps say where. What is simulated: Finacle, Kafka, the notification service, NIMC/NIBSS verification, transfers, approvals and the Customer 360 actions. No SMS, email or WhatsApp is ever sent.
  • The screenshots were taken with every write to the tenant intercepted, so where a screen depends on a live case the step says what you see instead (for example the extraction review’s “sample read”).

What the customer told us

The BRD’s own statement of the problem (§3.1) and the pain points it measures (§6.2), and where the POC answers each. No call notes were available, so these are the bank’s words as written in the BRD.

1“Manual data keying from the AOP takes 15–30 minutes per account, with a high error rate and rework. (§6.2)”

An uploaded form is read by IXP inside the case and every field comes back with a confidence; the person only corrects what the model was unsure of. In bulk, the maker reviews the extraction side by side with the form instead of typing it.

Upload a form · Review an extraction

2“Sequential verification calls add 5–10 minutes of cycle time to each application. (§6.2)”

NIN, BVN, AML, PEP, fraud, TIN, CAC, sanctions, watchlist and adverse media run in parallel as case tasks; the maker sees them as already done when the application reaches them.

Batch monitor · Case detail

3“Visual cross-checking is time-consuming and error-prone. (§6.2)”

Reconciliation compares the form, NIBSS and NIMC before a person looks, and names the one field that disagrees — “AOP reads 14/03/1984, NIMC reads 14/03/1979”.

Reconciliation · Exception case

4“No real-time pipeline visibility for RMs — they cannot give customers a credible time-to-account. (§6.2)”

My desk ranks what needs the RM with the live Maestro stage on every row; the customer has their own status screen; management has the control tower.

My desk · Track (customer) · Control tower

5“Operations cannot scale beyond ~300 accounts a day without more staff. (§6.2)”

Exceptions are cleared by cause, in bulk, and a pattern of identical human decisions can be promoted to a rule so it stops reaching a person at all.

Triage by cause · Promote a rule
